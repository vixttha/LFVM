# Research library laws

1. Separate template schema version from research data version; preserve prior editions and stable global record IDs.
2. Preserve existing free classes F0–F5, legitimacy A/B/C/U, access status, compute categories, billing risk R0–R4/RU and evidence E0–E6. Keep orthogonal dimensions separate; follow accepted dictionary.
3. Distinguish organizations, platforms, services, offers and resources; counts must use explicit entity types. Never count trials, closed, API-only or unverified offers as unrestricted free VMs.
4. Store typed, atomic, machine-readable data and explicit unknown/false/not applicable; one authoritative value per fact, reproducible derived fields.
5. Cite specific claims with source URL, retrieval date, quality and effective dates. Preserve contradictions and superseded facts; never claim testing or zero cost without evidence.
6. Billing, security, privacy, legitimacy and reliability are independent; document fees, credit expiry, hard caps, storage/network charges, renewal and workload restrictions.
7. Public-safe data only. No credentials, personal account details, private source links or executable imported text. Prevent spreadsheet/CSV formula injection; no macros, auto connections or prompt execution. Review publication safety.
8. Use accepted schema, validated keys and relationships, source reconciliation, freshness and duplicate checks. No hidden loss during migration; preserve original IDs/mapping.
9. Support logical expansion through template-defined partitions and machine-readable inventory with record counts and digests. Excel has finite physical capacity; never claim unlimited rows in one workbook.
10. Record exact artifact digests, validation evidence, differences, improvements, degradations, tradeoffs and unknowns. Standard free public GitHub runners only; no mandatory external service.
11. No automatic merge, release or modification of locked editions. Owner controls scope and acceptance.
